# S.E.N.T.I.N.E.L. Offline First Aid Statements & Medical Review

This document catalogues all 10 emergency first-aid protocols packaged in S.E.N.T.I.N.E.L. (`content/firstaid_en.json` and `content/firstaid_hi.json`) for clinical and human safety review.

All emergency guidance is developed in strict accordance with evidence-based first-aid principles established by:
- **International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies (IFRC)** International First Aid, Resuscitation, and Education Guidelines.
- **American Red Cross / American Heart Association (AHA)** First Aid Guidelines.
- **World Health Organization (WHO)** Emergency Trauma and Clinical Care Protocols.

---

## Universal Medical Safety Disclaimer
> **"Not a substitute for professional medical care. In an emergency, always call 112 or 108 immediately."**
> **"व्यावसायिक चिकित्सा देखभाल का विकल्प नहीं है। आपात स्थिति में, हमेशा तुरंत 112 या 108 पर कॉल करें।"**

## 1. CPR (Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation)
- **Clinical Category:** Adult / Child Sudden Cardiac Arrest
- **Summary:** Immediate emergency resuscitation for cardiac arrest and unresponsive victims.
- **When to Call 112/108:** Call 112 or 108 immediately if the person is unresponsive, unconscious, or not breathing normally.
- **Action Steps (Max 6, short sentences):**
  1. Check responsiveness by tapping the shoulders firmly and shouting loudly.
  2. Call emergency services (112 or 108) immediately and ask for an AED.
  3. Check for normal breathing or chest rise for no more than 10 seconds.
  4. Place heel of one hand in center of chest, other hand on top with interlocked fingers.
  5. Push hard and fast at 100–120 compressions per minute at 5–6 cm depth.
  6. Do not interrupt compressions until emergency medical help takes over or AED arrives.

## 2. Choking (Adult & Child)
- **Clinical Category:** Acute Foreign Body Airway Obstruction (FBAO)
- **Summary:** Emergency airway clearance for a conscious choking person unable to breathe.
- **When to Call 112/108:** Call 112 or 108 immediately if the obstruction does not clear within two cycles, or if the person becomes unresponsive.
- **Action Steps (Max 6, short sentences):**
  1. Ask 'Are you choking?' and encourage coughing if they are able to cough or speak.
  2. If unable to cough or breathe, lean them forward supporting their chest.
  3. Deliver 5 firm back blows between shoulder blades using the heel of your hand.
  4. If airway remains blocked, give 5 quick upward abdominal thrusts (Heimlich maneuver).
  5. For infants under 1 year, alternate 5 back slaps with 2-finger chest thrusts only.
  6. If the victim becomes unresponsive, lower to the ground and start CPR immediately.

## 3. Severe Bleeding
- **Clinical Category:** Major Vascular Hemorrhage & Hypovolemic Shock Prevention
- **Summary:** Rapid hemorrhage control to prevent life-threatening shock.
- **When to Call 112/108:** Call 112 or 108 immediately if bleeding spurts, will not stop after 5 minutes of direct pressure, or signs of shock appear.
- **Action Steps (Max 6, short sentences):**
  1. Protect yourself with clean gloves or a waterproof barrier if available.
  2. Apply firm, continuous direct pressure with sterile gauze or clean cloth directly over wound.
  3. Keep pressure constant for at least 5 minutes without lifting the cloth to check.
  4. If blood soaks through, add additional pads on top without removing original dressing.
  5. Elevate the injured limb above heart level only if no broken bones are suspected.
  6. If limb bleeding is uncontrollable, apply a commercial tourniquet 5–7 cm above the wound.

## 4. Burns (Thermal & Chemical)
- **Clinical Category:** Thermal, Chemical, and Electrical Burn Trauma
- **Summary:** Immediate cooling and protective care for thermal, chemical, or electrical burn injuries.
- **When to Call 112/108:** Call 112 or 108 immediately for burns larger than the palm, burns on face/airway/joints, electrical burns, or chemical exposure.
- **Action Steps (Max 6, short sentences):**
  1. Ensure personal scene safety and safely remove the heat source or chemical exposure.
  2. Cool the burn under clean, cool running water for at least 20 minutes.
  3. Gently remove rings, watches, and restrictive clothing before swelling develops.
  4. Do NOT apply ice, butter, oils, toothpaste, or adhesive bandages to burned skin.
  5. Loosely cover burned skin with a sterile non-stick dressing or clean plastic wrap.
  6. Keep the person warm to prevent hypothermia during prolonged cooling.

---

## 5. Fracture & Sprains
- **Clinical Category:** Musculoskeletal Trauma & Suspected Fractures
- **Summary:** Safe immobilization and trauma management for suspected bone or joint fractures.
- **When to Call 112/108:** Call 112 or 108 immediately if bone pierces the skin, limb is cold or blue, or head, neck, or spine injury is suspected.
- **Action Steps (Max 6, short sentences):**
  1. Instruct the injured person to remain completely still and avoid moving the injured area.
  2. Support the injured limb in the exact position found without attempting realignment.
  3. Never attempt to straighten a deformed limb or push an exposed bone back into flesh.
  4. Control any open wound bleeding with gentle pressure around the wound, avoiding the bone.
  5. Apply an ice pack wrapped in a cloth for 15 minutes at a time to reduce swelling.
  6. Immobilize with a splint only if transport is unavoidable and you are properly trained.

## 6. Snake Bite
- **Clinical Category:** Venomous Envenomation Protocol (Indian Subcontinent & Global)
- **Summary:** Crucial immobilization protocol for venomous snake puncture injuries.
- **When to Call 112/108:** Call 112 or 108 immediately for any venomous snake bite or puncture wound with swelling, pain, or dizziness.
- **Action Steps (Max 6, short sentences):**
  1. Keep the patient calm, lying down, and completely still to slow venom circulation.
  2. Remove rings, bangles, watches, and shoes immediately before rapid limb swelling occurs.
  3. Immobilize the bitten limb at or slightly below heart level with a splint.
  4. Apply a firm crepe pressure bandage from extremities toward torso if trained.
  5. Do NOT cut the wound, suck out venom, apply ice, or tie a tight arterial tourniquet.
  6. Note the snake's color and markings safely, and transport immediately to hospital.

## 7. Drowning & Flood Rescue
- **Clinical Category:** Submersion Asphyxia & Hypoxia
- **Summary:** Safe water extrication and emergency resuscitation for submersion victims.
- **When to Call 112/108:** Call 112 or 108 immediately for any submersion incident, even if the person appears to recover quickly.
- **Action Steps (Max 6, short sentences):**
  1. Ensure rescuer safety first; reach or throw a floating object, do not enter dangerous water.
  2. Remove victim from water safely while supporting their head and neck.
  3. Check responsiveness and normal breathing immediately once on dry ground.
  4. If not breathing, deliver 5 initial rescue breaths followed by 30 chest compressions (CPR).
  5. Continue cycles of 30 chest compressions and 2 rescue breaths until help takes over.
  6. If breathing normally, place in recovery position on their side and keep warm.

---

## 8. Heat Stroke
- **Clinical Category:** Severe Environmental Hyperthermia (> 40°C Core Temp)
- **Summary:** Emergency rapid whole-body cooling for life-threatening hyperthermia.
- **When to Call 112/108:** Call 112 or 108 immediately. Heat stroke (temperature over 40°C, confusion, rapid pulse) is an extreme medical emergency.
- **Action Steps (Max 6, short sentences):**
  1. Move the person immediately into dense shade or an air-conditioned room or vehicle.
  2. Remove heavy outer clothing to expose as much skin surface as possible.
  3. Rapidly cool the person by spraying cool water while actively fanning their skin.
  4. Apply cold wet cloths or ice packs to the neck, armpits, and groin.
  5. Do NOT force fluids if the person is confused, drowsy, vomiting, or unconscious.
  6. Monitor breathing and consciousness closely until emergency medical teams arrive.

## 9. Fainting (Syncope)
- **Clinical Category:** Transient Vasovagal or Orthostatic Cerebral Hypoperfusion
- **Summary:** Emergency management for temporary loss of consciousness due to reduced brain blood flow.
- **When to Call 112/108:** Call 112 or 108 immediately if the person remains unconscious for over 1 minute, has chest pain, or fell and struck their head.
- **Action Steps (Max 6, short sentences):**
  1. Lay the person flat on their back on the ground immediately.
  2. Elevate their feet and legs 20–30 cm above heart level to restore blood flow to brain.
  3. Loosen tight collars, ties, belts, or restrictive clothing around the neck.
  4. Ensure fresh airflow by opening windows and asking onlookers to step back.
  5. If they do not regain consciousness within 1 minute, roll into recovery position and check breathing.
  6. Do not allow them to stand up quickly once awake; have them rest lying down for 10 minutes.

---

## 10. Poisoning & Ingestion
- **Clinical Category:** Toxic Ingestion, Pesticide, and Chemical Inhalation
- **Summary:** Immediate toxicity response for ingested chemicals, pesticides, or toxic substances.
- **When to Call 112/108:** Call 112 or 108 immediately, or call the National Poison Information Centre (1800-116-117) with the product details.
- **Action Steps (Max 6, short sentences):**
  1. Ensure personal safety and remove the victim from toxic fumes or chemical contact area.
  2. Safely identify the toxic container, chemical label, plant, or medication involved.
  3. Do NOT induce vomiting unless specifically instructed by poison control or emergency doctors.
  4. If the substance is on skin or in eyes, flush continuously with clean water for 15 minutes.
  5. If unconscious but breathing normally, place in the recovery position on their side.
  6. Bring the chemical container or substance sample with the patient to the hospital.
